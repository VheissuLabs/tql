v0.13.1

The SQL editor on demand, and a settings screen that is easier to type into.

New Features

- Show or hide the SQL editor from the ctrl+k palette, or with | (shift and \,
  next to \ for the table list). Showing it opens the editor with your last
  statement; hiding it says which key brings it back. With sql_always on,
  hiding it holds until you show it again or quit, and your setting is left
  as it is. The key can be rebound as toggle_sql.

Enhancements

- Editing a setting starts with its value selected: type to replace it,
  backspace to clear it, or ← and → to keep it and edit in place.
- The palette lists tables, rows, reload and query history once each, as the
  keyed action with its shortcut, instead of a second time as a : command.

Bug Fixes

- Editing a setting shows your terminal's own cursor after the value, on a row
  that keeps its highlight, so it can be seen whatever your theme colours. The
  value is underlined while typing would replace it.
- The record inspector lines up related rows and column types that hold wide
  characters such as Chinese, Japanese and emoji, measuring them by the
  columns they take on screen.
- A setting's description breaks a word too long for the line instead of
  running past the edge.
